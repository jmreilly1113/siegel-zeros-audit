Abstract
OpenAI’s openai/math release includes a Lean formalization of “Uniform exclusion
of Landau–Siegel zeros” (OpenAI, October 1, 2026). Its Comparator challenge has
two checked theorems, whose statements we prove equivalent in Lean. OpenAI proves
them by two routes, neither through the paper’s proof of Lemma 3. The catalogued
main result, exists_absolute_real_zero_gap, gets its spanning step from a local Bézout argument and does not use OpenAI’s Corollary 4 theorem. The second theorem,
dirichletRealZeroBound_proof, proves Corollary 4 by a multiplicity estimate. We formalized the paper’s proofs of Lemma 3 and Corollary 4 in Lean. By reading, the
Lean statements match the paper’s, and the declarations use only the axioms propext,
Classical.choice and Quot.sound. A +2/-1 substitution puts the paper’s Corollary 4
into dirichletRealZeroBound_proof. Comparator accepts the result with both kernels
at openai/math fd4aeeb under a deny list of all 45 socket address families, and that
theorem’s closure no longer contains the three named OpenAI lemmas. Our replay of
the corpus agrees with an independent replay on all 402 configurations where both have
a verdict.

Introduction

OpenAI’s openai/math repository publishes mathematical manuscripts together with Lean
formalizations and Comparator challenges; we use it at commits adc7f12 and fd4aeeb [15, 16].
The manuscript “Uniform exclusion of Landau–Siegel zeros” (OpenAI, October 1, 2026) [18]
states as its Theorem 1 that there is an absolute constant c > 0 such that every real zero
beta in (0,1) of every primitive nonprincipal real Dirichlet L-function of conductor q >= 3
satisfies (1 - beta) log q >= c. Comparator’s README [7] states that for each listed theorem
it guarantees three things: the theorem proves the statement given in the challenge, uses no
more axioms than permitted, and is accepted by the Lean kernel. Comparator can also pass
the solution export to an external kernel; we used nanoda [1], at the official build 3a24072,
as the second kernel.
Acceptance shows that a Lean proof of the Lean statement exists, using only the permitted axioms. It does not show that the Lean proof follows the paper’s argument: in
the case studied here, both accepted OpenAI proofs obtain the paper’s spanning step by
other means (Section 4). Bastounis, Circelli and Hansen [3] argue that a Lean check of an
AI autoformalisation need not certify the natural-language proof, and show for OpenAI’s
Navier–Stokes formalisation that the Lean proof does not correspond to the natural-language
proof. Their Section 1 states that they make no claims about the correctness of OpenAI’s
natural-language proof.
We find that the challenge has two checked theorems, with equivalent statements, and
that OpenAI proves them by two routes. OpenAI’s catalogued main result, exists_absolute_real_zero_gap,
gets its spanning step from a local Bézout statement, through a zero test with Corollary
4’s bounds, and does not use OpenAI’s Corollary 4 theorem. The second theorem, dirichletRealZeroBound_proof, proves Corollary 4 as actual_biquadratic_rectangle_span by a
multiplicity estimate, uniform_rectangular_multiplicity. So neither route goes through the
paper’s proof of Lemma 3.
Our contributions are these.
• Statement faithfulness of the four family-003 challenges, by reading the Lean statements against the papers’ texts, with Lean lemmas showing the SiegelZeros challenge
is not vacuous (Section 3, with the reasoning behind each reading in Tables 2–9).
• Lean proofs of the paper’s Lemma 3 and Corollary 4, with statements that match the
paper’s and only the standard axioms (Section 5).
• A reroute of dirichletRealZeroBound_proof through that Corollary 4, accepted by
Comparator with both kernels (Section 6).

Section 4 describes OpenAI’s two formal routes. Section 5 gives the Lean proofs of Lemma

Section 8 lists threats to validity, Section 9 the artifacts, and Section 10 the use of AI tools.

Background

2.1

Comparator and nanoda

Comparator’s README at commit d03acab [7] states three guarantees for each listed theorem of a solution: that it proves the same statement as provided in the challenge, that it
uses no more axioms than listed in permitted_axioms, and that it is accepted by the Lean
kernel. The same README states that external kernels registered in external_kernels are
given a file containing the solution export. According to Comparator’s and lean4export’s
READMEs at the pinned commits, both kernel checks depend on the Lake build of the solution in a landrun sandbox, on the lean4export export of the solution, and on Comparator’s
own statement and axiom checks. By the same READMEs, only the Lean kernel’s check
replays the solution environment inside the Comparator process, and only nanoda’s check
runs as a separate process given a file containing the solution export and a nanoda-style
config.json. Comparator’s README states the guarantees under six assumptions, among
them that “The Lean kernel is correct (with ‘external_kernels‘ this can be reduced to "At
least one of the Lean kernel or the ‘external_kernels‘ is correct")”. nanoda’s README at
commit 3a24072 [1] states that nanoda_bin takes one argument, a JSON configuration file,
and reads an export file created by lean4export.
All runs used Lean 4.34.1 (5045d005), Mathlib d13f23b, Comparator v4.34.0 d03acab and
lean4export v4.34.0 076e8e5 [8, 10, 7, 9]. The full commits are d03acab154d269c06e60e4de7e4cc85deebff94b
for Comparator and 076e8e57707e813375e8f9da8bf989799ace9680 for lean4export. The official nanoda is 3a24072; f67473b, 37cb87b and 37cb87b with nealmcb’s patch at 64 MiB are
unofficial builds, reported separately.

The paper’s lemmas

The paper [18] states its Theorem 1 at refs/siegel-paper.tex:66-73, Lemma 2 at 181-194,
Lemma 3 at 252-267, Corollary 4 at 410-420, Lemma 6 at 517-524 and Lemma 7 at 598604. The paper numbers all of these with one counter, so the result between Corollary 4 and
Lemma 6 is Remark 5. Lemma 2 gives a prime-sum bound for X >= 3 and a lower bound for
the mass of primes with chi(p) = -1. Lemma 3 states that, for a surjective linear map A from
C^4 to C^3 whose kernel is spanned by a vector with Q-linearly independent coordinates,
evaluation at A n for n in E_N is surjective from P_t under the budget (3.1). The paper
cites Fischler [4] as [Fischler, Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 1.3] at refs/siegel-paper.tex:155.
Corollary 4 states that, under the hypotheses of Lemma 3, the monomial rows with alpha_j
<= T_j span C^(E_N). Lemma 6 bounds the exponent sums S_1 and S_2, and Lemma 7
states the divisibility of Delta by p^(E_p) for every admissible prime p.

The paper states its Theorem 1 at refs/siegel-paper.tex:66-73. Its text, lines 67-72, reads:
There is an absolute constant c > 0 such that every real zero β ∈ (0, 1) of every
primitive nonprincipal real Dirichlet L-function of conductor q ≥ 3 satisfies
(1 − β) log q ≥ c.

(1.1)

Four Lean lemmas show that the hypotheses of the challenge can be met and what
the statement specialises to. NonVacuity.hypotheses_satisfiable proves that for every odd
prime p the quadratic character mod p satisfies all hypotheses of the challenge (q = p ≥ 3,
primitive, nonprincipal, real). NonVacuity.quadChar_LFunction_eq_LSeries proves that
the L-function of the quadratic character equals its Dirichlet series for Re s > 1. NonVacuity.quadChar_LFunction_one_ne_zero proves L(1, χp ) ̸= 0 for the quadratic character
mod an odd prime p. NonVacuity.specialization proves that the challenge theorem specialises
to an explicit statement about the quadratic characters. Each of the four depends on the
axioms [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]. 1

Threats to validity

Kernel trust. Comparator’s README states its guarantees under six assumptions, among
them that “The Lean kernel is correct (with ‘external_kernels‘ this can be reduced to "At
least one of the Lean kernel or the ‘external_kernels‘ is correct")”. By Comparator’s and
lean4export’s READMEs, both kernel checks depend on the same Lake build and the same
lean4export export, while only the Lean kernel’s check runs inside the Comparator process.
Two kernels therefore reduce the risk of a kernel bug but not of an exporter or build bug.
What agreement does not test. Our replay agrees with an independent replay on all 402
configurations where both have a verdict. Every outcome on both sides of that comparison
was acceptance, so the comparison does not test rejection. Rejection is tested by negative
controls. The standalone nanoda check rejects the Lemma 3 export with Classical.choice not
permitted (exit 1), and rejects it with rectangle’s proof replaced by interpolation’s proof (exit
101). Comparator rejects the SiegelZeros solution with Classical.choice not permitted (Illegal
axiom detected, exit 1) and with the challenge statement altered (statement mismatch, exit
1).
Statement faithfulness is by reading. The six statement matches rest on reading,
signed off by the author, and the reasoning behind them is recorded point by point, also
signed off. The non-vacuity lemmas rule out a vacuous challenge but do not establish
faithfulness. The HeckeSevenEighths match rests partly on Lean lemmas (the lattice series
for Re s > 1 and holomorphy off 0, 1) and partly on reading; the six-generator identification
itself is not formalized in Lean.
Scope of the reroute. The reroute changes one of the two checked theorems, dirichletRealZeroBound_proof. “Only use” of actual_biquadratic_rectangle_span holds within
the SiegelZeros tree. The separate copy in namespace OAI.Erdos970 is measured at import level only, for 3 of 416 configurations. The dependency scan matches five names
(uniform_rectangular_multiplicity, source_rectangle_span_of_polynomial_zero_test, actual_biquadratic_rectangle_span, actual_biquadratic_rectangle_span_via_lemma3, interpolation), so “no longer contains” applies to those names only.
Dependencies. The rerouted chain still imports OpenAI’s patched PrimeNumberTheoremAnd.SiegelZeros.HadamardSupport, whose correctness is outside this work.
Sandbox. The primary results ran under a deny list naming all 45 address families of
kernel 5.15.167.4, under which all 138 socket() calls of an all-families probe fail. Earlier
runs used a 5-family filter, which denied IP, Unix-domain, netlink and packet sockets but
left AF_VSOCK open, with a connection to the Windows host succeeding, and also left
AF_PPPOX and AF_ALG creatable [6]. Three runs had no network filter: the NonVacuity
lemmas, the standalone nanoda run and the statement-check build. None of those three is
a run where a filter affects the result.
