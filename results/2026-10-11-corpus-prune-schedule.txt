## Reading (INFERRED)
- Peak WSL disk use: about 50.9 GiB, set by the headline batch (current 39 GiB after deleting mainsrc, plus about 10 GiB of
  headline builds and the 36 package modules). No remainder batch exceeds it: remainder peaks are 44.0-50.7 GiB.
- Chosen schedule: 15 remainder batches (budget 10 GiB of new build output per batch), after the headline batch. Smaller
  budgets give the same peak with more batches; larger budgets raise the peak (15 GiB: 55.6, 20: 60.2, 30: 70.1).
  Without pruning, building everything would need about 176.5 GiB.
- Batch order: greedy, so configs that share modules with what is already present go first. That is why batch 1 has
  81 small configs and the last batches hold single large ones (DegreeRigidity 5.5 GiB, PartitionPrinciple 5.2, FreeIsing 4.9).
  The greedy order is a heuristic, not a proven optimum.
- What stays between batches: the pilot closures (SiegelZeros, QuasiRiemannHypothesis, EditApproximation) and every OAI module a later
  batch needs. Package builds (Mathlib and the rest) are never pruned. Pruning happens only after a batch's shipped-config
  reruns and queries finish.
- C:: the WSL disk image (ext4.vhdx, 43.1 GB, not sparse) grows when use inside WSL exceeds what it already holds, and it
  does not shrink on its own when files are deleted. Deleted space is reused inside the image. A peak of about 51 GiB
  inside therefore means the image grows to roughly 51-53 GiB, taking about 8-10 GB more of C:, which had 57.3 GB free on 2026-10-11 20:5x.
  Returning space to C: afterwards would need a manual compaction of the image (not planned).
- Uncertainty: unbuilt sizes come from a fit with R^2 0.84 (per-module scatter is large, the summed fit error on built
  modules is about 0%). Treat each peak as roughly +/- 2-3 GiB. The guard cap for the remainder should stay well above 51 GiB.
